The Placement Eligibility Checker automates how student records are evaluated against placement job descriptions while keeping verification transparent and auditable. Instead of relying solely on an unverified language model to decide who gets shortlisted, the system combines structured information extraction with deterministic rule checks and explicit review states.

In this pipeline, job descriptions from jobs.csv are parsed into a five-field JSON schema (branches, min_cgpa, max_backlogs, batch, and summary) guided by system_prompt.txt, while student records are loaded from students.csv. Because raw job postings often contain ambiguous phrasing, missing limits, or grading scales that differ from CGPA, every extracted field passes through check_facts() before eligibility is evaluated. If required criteria cannot be verified against the post, the job is marked needs_review rather than guessed. Only jobs with a matched status are evaluated against student eligibility rules (is_eligible) and written into eligible.csv and job_summary.csv, alongside review.csv and summary.txt. Additional modules extend the workflow with priority ranking (priority_report.csv), skill-gap analysis against company_skills.csv (skill_gap_report.csv), and duplicate resume detection.

Using AI helped translate unstructured job text into candidate structured fields quickly, reducing manual transcription effort. However, deterministic rules and fact-checking proved essential because language models can over-generalize, infer unsupported thresholds, or misinterpret phrasing when rules are implicit. A key lesson from debugging and edge-case testing was treating unverified model output as untrusted until validated against source text. Handling edge cases—such as ensuring branch rules do not assume "ALL" without explicit wording, refusing silent percentage-to-CGPA conversions, resolving special job-handling quirks like J08, and checking that skill-gap percentages and duplicate resume checks behave consistently—highlighted how small parsing assumptions can distort downstream shortlists.

Responsible AI engineering in high-stakes workflows like campus placements requires clear boundaries between probabilistic assistance and deterministic accountability. When information is missing or ambiguous, routing items to human review (needs_review) is safer than fabricating certainty. Documenting why a student is ineligible or why a job needs review makes automated screening easier to audit, challenge, and improve. Overall, building this system reinforced that reliable AI applications depend less on model cleverness alone and more on structured schemas, factual verification, predictable fallback behavior, and human oversight where judgment matters most.
